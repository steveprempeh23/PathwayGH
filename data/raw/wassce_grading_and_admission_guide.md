# WASSCE Grading System

The West African Senior School Certificate Examination (WASSCE) uses a standardized
nine-point grading scale. Grades A1 through C6 are credit passes; D7 and E8 are
passing grades but are not credits and do not count toward tertiary admission
eligibility; F9 is a fail.

| Grade | Points | Interpretation         | Counts as credit? |
|-------|--------|-------------------------|--------------------|
| A1    | 1      | Excellent               | Yes |
| B2    | 2      | Very Good               | Yes |
| B3    | 3      | Good                    | Yes |
| C4    | 4      | Credit (high average)   | Yes |
| C5    | 5      | Credit (average)        | Yes |
| C6    | 6      | Credit (below average)  | Yes |
| D7    | 7      | Pass (not a credit)     | No |
| E8    | 8      | Pass (not a credit)     | No |
| F9    | 9      | Fail                    | No |

Lower grade points are better. A candidate with six A1 grades has the best possible
aggregate (6); a candidate with six C6 grades has an aggregate of 36.

# How the Aggregate Is Calculated

The WASSCE aggregate is calculated from six subjects: three core subjects and three
elective subjects, using each candidate's best scores. The grade points for each of
the six subjects are summed to produce the aggregate.

Example: English (B3=3) + Core Mathematics (B2=2) + Integrated Science (C4=4) +
Elective 1 (B2=2) + Elective 2 (B3=3) + Elective 3 (C4=4) = Aggregate 18.

## Core Subject Rules

The core subjects used depend on the track:

- **Science-related disciplines**: English Language, Core Mathematics, and
  Integrated Science (or Core Science) are used as the core subjects. Social
  Studies credit is excluded from the calculation for these disciplines.
- **Non-science disciplines** (Arts, Business, Humanities): English Language,
  Core Mathematics, and Social Studies (or Life Skills) are used as the core
  subjects. Integrated Science credit is excluded from the calculation for
  these disciplines.

## Mandatory Credit Gate

Regardless of the total aggregate, a candidate must score a credit pass (C6 or
better) in English Language, Core Mathematics, and the relevant third core
subject (Integrated Science or Social Studies, depending on track). A candidate
can have a numerically low aggregate overall and still be disqualified from
public-university admission if any one of these three subjects falls below C6 —
for example, a candidate with A1s in three electives and Social Studies but a
D7 in Core Mathematics can compute an aggregate of 12 on paper and still be
rejected, because the credit gate on Core Mathematics is not met.

# Admission Aggregate Bands by Programme Category

The following bands are general public-university admission guidance and are
illustrative, not a live or authoritative cutoff feed. Actual cutoffs are
published per institution per admission cycle and can vary from these bands.

- **Medicine / Dentistry** (University of Ghana, KNUST, UCC): aggregate 6 to 10.
- **Engineering, Pharmacy, Actuarial Science**: aggregate 6 to 18.
- **General Science disciplines** (non-clinical): aggregate roughly 6 to 24.
- **Business, Finance, Accounting**: aggregate roughly 6 to 24.
- **General Humanities, Social Sciences, Education**: aggregate up to 24.
- **Open public-university entry** (general eligibility floor): aggregate up to 36.
- **HND / Polytechnic (Technical University) tracks**: aggregate up to 48, with
  a required credit in English Language and Core Mathematics.

# Typical Elective Subject Requirements by Programme Category

The aggregate bands above say how low an aggregate needs to be; they do not
say which three elective subjects actually count toward it for a given
programme. Public universities generally require the three electives to be
*relevant to the intended programme*, not just any three credit passes.
The combinations below are the commonly published, typical elective
requirements for each category - always confirm against the specific
institution and programme, since individual departments can add further
requirements (for example, a specific pass mark in Elective Mathematics).

## Business, Finance, Accounting

Typical relevant electives: Economics, Elective (Further) Mathematics,
Financial Accounting (also called Principles of Accounts), Business
Management, and Costing. A candidate most commonly presents three of these
five, often Economics plus two of the remaining four. General Mathematics
counts as a core subject, not an elective, for this category - Elective
Mathematics is a separate subject and is not required everywhere, but is
commonly preferred for Accounting and Finance-focused programmes
specifically, more so than for a general Business Administration track.

## Engineering, Pharmacy, Actuarial Science

Typical relevant electives: Physics, Chemistry, and Elective Mathematics.
These three are the standard combination expected for Engineering and
Pharmacy programmes specifically. Technical or vocational electives (for
example Technical Drawing, Applied Electricity, Electronics) are not
normally accepted as substitutes for Physics, Chemistry, and Elective
Mathematics at university-level Engineering or Pharmacy admission, even
though they are themselves legitimate electives for Technical University
/ HND tracks in the same broad subject area.

## General Science (non-clinical)

Typical relevant electives: any three of Physics, Chemistry, Biology, and
Elective Mathematics, chosen based on the specific science programme
(for example, Biology and Chemistry are typically expected for
Biological Sciences; Physics and Elective Mathematics for Physical
Sciences or Computer Science).

## General Humanities, Social Sciences, Education

Typical relevant electives vary more widely by specific programme than
in the science-based categories above, since "Humanities" covers many
distinct subject areas (Law, Sociology, Political Science, Literature,
History, French, and others). Commonly accepted electives include
Government, Economics, Literature in English, French, History,
Geography, and Christian or Islamic Religious Studies - a candidate
typically presents three electives drawn from subjects relevant to their
specific intended programme rather than one fixed combination for the
whole category.

## Physician Assistantship / Allied Health

Typical relevant electives: Biology, Chemistry, and Physics (or
Elective Mathematics in place of Physics at some institutions). This
combination is close to, but not always identical to, the Engineering
combination above - the two categories are commonly confused in casual
questions, which is itself one reason an eligibility check should name
the specific category it checked against, not just say "Science."

# University of Ghana Specific Requirements

For admission to a full-time undergraduate degree programme at the University of
Ghana, an applicant must have credit passes (A1–C6) in:

- The four core subjects: English Language, Core Mathematics, Integrated
  Science or Core Science, and Social Studies.
- Three elective subjects relevant to the intended programme.

The total aggregate of the six relevant subjects (core and elective) must not
exceed aggregate 24 for regular admission, or aggregate 30 for Distance
Education admission.

# What to Do if an Aggregate Falls Short

If a candidate's aggregate is higher (worse) than the cutoff for their
first-choice programme, WAEC and the relevant admissions bodies note several
options:
- The candidate may still be considered for other, less competitive programmes
  with different entry requirements.
- The candidate may resit individual subjects in a later WASSCE sitting or
  through the WASSCE for Private Candidates track to improve specific grades
  before reapplying in a future admission cycle.

A calculated aggregate should always be treated as a provisional estimate until
confirmed against the official cutoff points published by the target
institution for that specific admission cycle.
